How to use this document
The main NolMart brand sells electronics and accessories: oraimo products, phones and other items found in Kariakoo. You do not hold stock, so this calendar is built around two goals: build trust, and show what you can order for people, so sales follow.
Every day has its hook, message and call to action. Every picture, carousel slide and video made with AI has a full prompt that restates the whole brand specification, followed by a ready-to-paste caption with its five hashtags. Two days are real-photo days, and they come with a shooting guide and a caption instead of a prompt.
Copy one prompt box into ChatGPT (images) or Google Flow (videos), then copy the green caption box when you post.

1.2 The Honest Way to Say It
You do not hold stock, and that is fine, as long as you say it plainly. Hiding it is what creates distrust. The whole calendar uses one simple sentence: "Lipa kwanza, tunakununulia na kukuletea" (pay first, we buy it for you and deliver it). Say it in Day 1, explain it in Days 2 and 10, and prove it in Days 20 and 24.
1.3 Ten Ways This Calendar Builds Trust Without Stock
* Say how it works openly (Days 1, 2 and 10), so no one feels tricked after paying.
* Show where products come from (Days 6 and 20), so the supply chain feels real.
* Teach something true and useful (Days 4, 8, 11, 15, 16, 22 and 25), because people trust those who know the subject.
* Teach people how to judge any online shop (Day 18), and then follow your own rules.
* Make three small promises and keep them (Day 14), then update every customer at each step on WhatsApp.
* Use real proof only for proof (Days 20 and 24): real photos of real orders, shared with permission.
* Answer questions in public (Days 7, 21 and 28), so new visitors see that a real person replies.
* Show products with the official product photo so the image matches what the customer will receive.
* Let people ask any question before paying (Days 6, 10 and 20).
* Use only confirmed prices and real delivery times, never a countdown or a fake "only 2 left".
1.4 The Order Flow (the Same Every Time)
1. The customer comments a keyword (ORAIMO, SIMU or ORDER) or sends a message.
2. You reply with the price list or the exact product details, and confirm the product, price and delivery time in writing.
3. The customer pays first, to your official payment number.
4. You buy the exact product (from the oraimo supplier, a phone dealer or Kariakoo).
5. You send an update at each step: paid, bought, on the way, delivered.
6. After delivery, ask the customer for permission to share a photo or message as proof.

	3.4 Making a Carousel Look Like One Set
1. In a new ChatGPT chat, attach the logo and paste the prompt for slide 1 (the cover). Regenerate until you love it.
2. In the same chat, attach slide 1 and the logo, then paste the prompt for slide 2. Repeat for every slide.
3. Check each slide against slide 1. If the background, blue shade, text style or logo position drifts, regenerate that slide.
4. Start a new chat for every new carousel.
5. AI text can contain spelling errors. If a word is wrong, regenerate once, or delete that word from the prompt and add it yourself in Canva or the Instagram editor.
6. If ChatGPT limits your uploads on the free plan, delete the LOGO paragraph from the prompts and add the logo to all slides at once in Canva afterward.
3.5 Video Rules (What Worked in Your Tests)
* Every video is one 10-second video made from one prompt. Do not continue a video from the last frame of another.
* Product studio videos: one product, a plain dark background, no people, one slow camera move per scene, and only light elements moving. These worked well in Google Flow and kept the product consistent.
* Flat explainer videos: simple shapes, no text inside the video. Add the on-screen words and the logo in CapCut or the app editor, because text inside AI video is unreliable.
* Check every take for three things: the product looks identical in every scene, nothing passes in front of the product, and the ending leaves clean empty space for your text.
* Free-plan planning: make videos in short sessions, and keep one spare attempt in each session in case a take fails.
3.6 Fixing Common AI Problems
Problem
	What to do
	Misspelled text
	Regenerate once. If it is still wrong, delete that word from the prompt and add it yourself in Canva or the Instagram editor.
	The product changes shape or color
	Attach the product photo again and add the sentence: "Keep the product exactly the same as in the attached photo."
	A person or hand appears
	Add at the start and at the end of the prompt: "No people, no faces and no hands."
	Text appears inside a video
	Add: "No text, letters, numbers or logos anywhere in the video," and add your words in CapCut.
	Colors drift
	Regenerate and keep the hex color codes in the prompt.
	The logo is redrawn or misspelled
	Delete the LOGO paragraph and add the real logo yourself in Canva.
	The video ends in a different place than planned
	Rewrite the ENDING line (or the last scene) in one sentence describing the exact final pose.

	6. Claims to Avoid and Rules to Follow
* Never say "in stock", "ready now" or "only a few left" unless it is true at that moment. Say "order today, we buy it for you and deliver it".
* Never present an AI image as proof of an order or as a photo of a real customer. AI images are for ideas and product spotlights. Only real photos prove real orders.
* Use the official product photo for Product Spotlight images and videos, so the picture matches what the customer receives.
* Say "genuine", "original" or "warranty" only if your supplier confirms it in writing, and state the warranty exactly as the supplier gives it.
* Publish only confirmed prices. Fill every [BEI] and [PRICE] with the real price before posting, and re-check it if the supplier price changes.
* Always tell the customer the product, the price and the delivery time in writing before they pay.
* Customer messages, names, photos and payment details: share only with permission, and hide private details.
* Tech facts: only post facts you can explain. The facts in this calendar are general and simplified, such as the 60 to 70 percent power bank figure, so keep the words "karibu" (about) and "kwa kawaida" (usually).
* Giveaway (Day 27): free to enter with no purchase needed, a clear closing time, a line saying the platform is not involved, the prize delivered as promised, and a check of local promotion rules.
* Do not use designer or competitor brand names in your own claims. The only brand you name is oraimo, and only for products you can really source from the oraimo supplier.
Details to confirm before you start
* Delivery area (Dar es Salaam only, or across Tanzania) and the normal delivery time.
* Your payment methods and the official payment number and business name customers will see.
* That you will send an update at every step, as promised on Days 2 and 14.
* Your refund or cancellation rule if a product cannot be found after payment.
* Which oraimo and phone models you can really source, and their confirmed prices, for the ManyChat price lists.
* Whether the oraimo supplier allows you to use the official product photos.
* The giveaway prize, closing date and time (Day 27).
